When an encounter begins, it's time for INITIATIVE!
#### Traditional Style
Traditional initiative has each participating party roll 1d12 + [[Observation|Perception]], with enemies winning ties. The result of the roll is their Initiative for the encounter - and the round begins with the highest, moving progressively down and ending with the lowest.
## Down In Initiative
If a creature would become incapacitated, dead, or otherwise OUT during an encounter, its initiative (and death saves) are moved to going BEFORE whatever cause that to happen.
# Ambush & Surprise
Ambushes and surprise attacks will happen often enough that their handling should be codified. In this, there are two parties – the Ambushers and the Ambushed. 

For the Ambushers to successfully pull off a surprise attack, they need to be Undetected by all the creatures they are trying to ambush – the Ambushed. This means that none of the Ambushed can be aware or suspicious of their Ambusher’s presence in their vicinity.

So, the Ambushers are Undetected and now attacking the Ambushed. Roll initiative!
- If an Ambusher would be Seen or Concealed, they are treated as Hidden by Ambushed creatures.
- An Ambushed creature cannot take reactions.
- Whenever an Ambushed creature begins its first turn, it is no longer Ambushed.
# Other Skills for Initiative
It’s entirely feasible that other skills can be used for Initiative besides Perception. The most common one is Sneak – a creature that is hiding when combat begins could choose to use their Sneak skill instead of other stuff. A person riding in on a mount could use their Riding skill for Initiative, a smooth-talking duelist may attempt to use a Social Skill for his initiative – and so on. When you get to it, it’ll make sense.
