Doom is the weight pressing back against the [[1.1 Player Characters|PC]]s as they take risks, act with [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Daring]], and push deeper into danger. It reflects the rising hostility of the battlefield - as the PCs strut grows more confident and powerful, the world can sense the tension and rachets up the pain in return.

Mechanically, [[1.3 Doom|Doom]] is an ambient gauge of how sharply the encounter is tilting toward catastrophe. The worse it gets, more the battle stalls and gnarls, the higher the [[1.3 Doom|Doom Level]] rises.
- [[1.3 Doom|Doom]] applies at all times, but resets on a per-encounter basis. 
- The ambient [[1.3 Doom|Doom Level]] is always equal to the highest amount of banked [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]]s a [[1.1 Player Characters|PC]] has at the moment (all PCs should have the same amount though).
- [[1.3 Doom|Doom Level]] ranges from 1 to 9.
Doom can never rise above 9. At high Doom, the party must spend their [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Daring]] well and have even better tactics.
## <font color="#d83931">Gaining Doom</font>
Increase Doom by **1** whenever any of the following occur:
- **The party stalls.**  
    A full round passes without an enemy being downed or meaningfully disabled, without useful forced movement, and/or without progress toward the encounter’s objective.
- **The party falters.**  
    A PC suffers a [[3. Breaking & Dying|Breakdown]] or begins [[3. Breaking & Dying|Dying]].
- **The [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Daring]] burns hot.**  
    A PC spends **3 or more Daring** during their turn.
- **A major setback hits.**  
    Terrain collapses, reinforcements arrive, an enemy evolves, or any other dramatic shift escalates the encounter’s tension. GMs - this should never happen more than twice in an encounter, be careful with it.
- **(Optional: GM Tool)**  
    When an enemy ability explicitly increases Doom.
## <font color="#d83931">Reducing Doom</font>
Rarer than its increase, but still possible. Doom drops whenever PCs make an advance that their enemies can't ignore. Here are some example situations that legitimately justify a Doom decrease. Use them sparingly - these are big-turn events, not routine successes.
- **A significant enemy is killed or incapacitated.**
	- Removing a boss phase, lieutenant, spellcaster, or encounter anchor can knock the fight’s tension down a notch.
-  **• An encounter objective is strongly advanced.**
	- Destroying a ritual focus, sealing a breach, collapsing a tunnel, capturing a device, or securing a hostage—all of these count as “the PCs actually changed the situation.”
- **PCs execute a powerful coordinated maneuver.**
	- A combo move, a chain of team actions, a multi-character setup that swings the board—if the table gasps or cheers, Doom has room to fall.
-  **A monster’s reinforcements are cut off or neutralized.**
	- If the PCs prevent incoming foes, or take out a mechanism that would escalate the encounter, Doom can ease.
- **The PCs reveal hidden leverage:**
	- breaking an artifact
	- invoking a powerful NPC favor
	- using environment hazards to huge effect
	- flipping the battlefield (fire, collapse, flood)
- **A boss loses a form or ability that defined the encounter’s rising pressure**. 
	- If the animating principle of the fight changes, Doom can fall to reflect that shift.
## <font color="#d83931">Effects of Doom</font>
Doom represents the encounter becoming more dangerous, volatile, and punishing.  
Enemies draw on rising [[1.3 Doom|Doom]] as instinct, fury, discipline, or supernatural force - whatever expresses the scene the best.

For each level of [[1.3 Doom|Doom]], there is a global +1 to [[2.1 Damage|Damage]] and [[2.2 Stress|Stress]] suffered by the [[1.1 Player Characters|PC]]s from all sources.

Every enemy may additionally unique [[1.3 Doom|Doom]] abilities on their monster profile.
