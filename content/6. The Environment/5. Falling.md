# <font color="#d83931">Falling</font>

When there is nothing beneath you—missing a ledge, slipping, jumping the wrong distance—you fall. Falling is dangerous because it combines speed, height, and impact force.

## What Happens When You Fall

A fall can be summarized as:

> **A creature moving at high speed hits the ground. Impact causes damage based on size and distance fallen.**

Creatures only suffer fall damage if they pass the **Distance Threshold** for their size category.

### Fall Speed & Damage

A falling creature accelerates until reaching its freefall speed.

|**Size**|**Freefall Speed (ft/s)**|**Feet Fallen per Combat Round**|**Distance Threshold**|**Fall Damage**|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|Diminutive|7|70 ft|30 ft|1 per 15 ft|
|Tiny|15|150 ft|5 ft|1d4 per 10 ft|
|Small|30|300 ft|10 ft|1d6 per 5 ft|
|Medium|40|400 ft|10 ft|1d6 per 5 ft|
|Large|50|500 ft|20 ft|2d4 per 5 ft|
|Huge|70|700 ft|25 ft|2d6 per 5 ft|
|Gargantuan|85|850 ft|40 ft|2d8 per 5 ft|
|Colossal|100|1000 ft|50 ft|2d12 per 5 ft|

Fall damage **cannot** be reduced by armor, resistances, or typical mitigation. It still passes through Vigor before causing Injury.

### The Falling Process

All creatures finish falling at:

- the **end of a skirmish round**,
    
- the **beginning of their next turn**,
    
- or when they reach a surface,
    

…depending on how far they had to fall.

If a creature would fall a distance **equal to or less than its Freefall Speed**, the fall resolves instantly.

The GM is the final arbiter of where a creature is, mid-fall, when external factors intervene (wind, ledges, spells, grapples, etc.).
