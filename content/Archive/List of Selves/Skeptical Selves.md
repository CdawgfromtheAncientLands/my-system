# **Trust Must Be Earned**

You want to have faith in people (or maybe you don't), but can only bear to give trust when you're sure the recipient won't use it as a knife. Consistency, honesty, sacrifice, loyalty: these are the currencies that matter to you. Early gestures of warmth feel suspicious, or even predatory. You test people quietly, open up slowly, and close down fast the moment someone lies, manipulates, or breaks rhythm. Trust is precious because it’s dangerous.

_People may call you guarded, perceptive, distant, or paranoid._

• _“Show me something real.”_  
• _“Everyone’s honest until they want something.”_  
• _“Trust comes last, not first.”_

# **I Need the Facts**

Walking into the unknown is how you get blindsided. Motives, plans, histories - these matter, and you refuse to make a move without understanding the board. You gather information aggressively: questions, observations, notes, interrogations. You adapt quickly when facts shift, but uncertainty ties your stomach in knots. Intuition is fine - _if_ it’s backed by experience. Otherwise? Get the data.

_People may call you methodical, detached, pedantic, or thorough._

• _“I don't want to bet on this... we need to learn more.”_  
• _“So, this mark... any family? Friends? What's his job?”_   
• _“Do we know how many there are? Who's their leader? We can't fight without learning this first.”_

# **People Don't Change**

You track patterns, not apologies. Behavior repeats - it’s safer to assume that than to get blindsided. When a bad egg claims they’ve changed, you mock it, ignore it, or test it; rarely, when undeniable proof hits the table, you adjust. This belief gives you insulation and control, even if it isolates you. Arguments about redemption drag you into cold, circular facts and sputtering frustration.

_People may call you cynical, realistic, perceptive, or resigned._

• _“Patterns don’t lie.”_  
• _“They’ll do it again.”_  
• _“Hope’s nice. Don’t bet on it.”_
