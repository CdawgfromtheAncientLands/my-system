Certain ground is uneven, sticky, cluttered, or otherwise harder to cross. Such spaces are considered **Difficult Terrain**.
- Moving into or through **Difficult Terrain** costs **double** the normal movement.
- Spaces occupied by a friendly creature or low obstacles (knee-high barriers, debris piles, shallow rubble, etc.) typically count as Difficult Terrain.
## Slippery Ground
Slippery surfaces—grease, oil, loose scree, wet stone, ice—can be treated as Difficult Terrain, but also risk knocking creatures [[Condition|Prone]]. The rules are as follows:

Once per [[2. Action & Effect Traits|{Move}]] action, at the first point of contact with Kind of Slippery ground, you may:
- Treat the space as **Difficult Terrain**, halving the speed at which you move through it.
- Attempt a [[Core Skills|Reflex]] or [[Finesse]] check (DC 17). On a failure, fall [[Condition|Prone]] and your [[2. Action & Effect Traits|{Move}]] action fails. On a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Crit Fail]], the GM may have you slide around.

The DC may change depending on the nature of the spell effect, item, or environment. 17 is the base for something like a wet shower floor or icy pavement. The GM may allow certain footwear OR the usage of bare feet to influence the mechanics of Slippery Ground.
