# When I Break Down...
So, you've failed your [[3.4 Testing Composure|Composure Check]] and suffered a [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Breakdown]]. First and foremost, you are past the point of doubt - **do not make [[3.4 Testing Composure|Composure Check]]s while you are suffering a [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Breakdown]].**

Then, you have two choices:
- Give in and perform a [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Desperate Act]],
- Or attempt to hold together and perform an [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Act of Will]].

---
## Desperate Acts
> *Your ally drops. For a split-second, your heart reaches for something - a plan, a callout... anything.*
> *What comes instead is a grim and simple clarity. Your chest tightens, all thoughts and reason choke out, and... you make your move.*

A *Desperate Act* is when you throw lesser concerns to the wind and impulsively react to the current situation - becoming violent, cowardly, paralyzed, or otherwise - for varying reasons related to your [[1.0 Character Identity|Identity]]. 
### So What The Hell Does This Mean, Game-Wise?
1. You must, as soon as possible, spend your
	 - [[1.3 Actions|Reaction ↻]] (yes, this means using a [[Impulses|Resolute Reaction (↻)]] if necessary)
	 - One [[1.1 Encounters|Turn]]'s worth of [[1.3 Actions|Action]]s (two if [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Peril]] is at 3+)
	Reacting unreasonably to the situation at hand in accordance with your [[1.0 Character Identity|Identity]]. Your [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Desperate Act]] lasts until you have used up all the required [[1.3 Actions|Action]]s.
2. During your [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Desperate Act]], you have +1 [[1.3 Advantage & Disadvantage|Advantage]] on all [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]]s.
3. You cannot take defensive, cautious, or disengaging actions unless they directly align with your [[1.0 Character Identity|Identity]].
4. As soon as your [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Desperate Act]] is over, become [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Shaken]] until the end of your next [[1.1 Encounters|Turn]]. If your total [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Peril]] is 3+, you are [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Shaken]] until your next rest.
##### Condition: <u>Shaken</u>
*You shudder as your body and mind register terrible shock. Nothing feels right.*
- You cannot benefit from any [[1.1 Skill Checks|Bonus]] or [[1.3 Advantage & Disadvantage|Advantage]] on a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]] or [[1.2 Disciplines & Skills|Skill DC]].
- You suffer [[2.6 Mental Conditions|Stupefied]] +X, where X is your total [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Peril]].

# Act of Will
Despite all your fervor and feeling, you may attempt to keep a cool head. Roll [[1.2 Disciplines & Skills|Nerve]] (DC = 11 + 2x current [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Peril]]). 

If you succeed:
- You remain in control, and temporarily steeled. Remove 1 tick of [[3.2 Mortal Peril|Mental Peril]].
- **Do not make [[3.4 Testing Composure|Composure Check]]s** until the end of your next [[1.1 Encounters|Turn]].

**If you fail:**
- Perform a [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Desperate Act]], becoming [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Shaken]] as usual afterward.
- The GM gives you a simple impulse, feeling, or command to carry out ("Run away", "Save your daughter", "Get that weapon away from him"). The GM will ensure that this always has negative or risky outcomes when they do so (you tempted fate, so now it bites back!).
- You still choose how those actions are carried out, but you cannot act against their direction.

# And Narratively?
Outside of structured encounters, the GM may ignore action tracking. An in-scene [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Breakdown]] is expressed and measured in behavior and acting. You may attempt an [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Act of Will]] as usual.

If you succeed on an [[3.5 Breakdown & Desperation|Act of Will]], you steady yourself. You may still choose to act emotionally or impulsively for dramatic effect (you are quite upset, after all), but you remain in control of your choices.

If you fail, you lose control. You act according to a clear, immediate impulse - fear, rage, desperation, obsession - until one of the following occurs:
- The situation meaningfully changes in a manner pleasing or satiating to your impulses
- You are physically or emotionally exhausted
- Someone successfully intervenes or talks you down

The GM may call for a small number of appropriate social checks to interrupt or redirect you, based on the intensity of the moment.

Desperate Acts in a narrative scene can be brief or extended:
- **Minor:** a single reckless decision, a sudden outburst, a moment of paralysis
- **Major:** sustained, dangerous behavior that escalates the scene or reshapes the narrative
# GM Nudging During Desperate Acts
A Desperate Act is a moment of lost control, but not a total loss of character ownership. An experienced or mischievous (both are good) GM may suggest impulses, escalations, or complications that heighten the scene, especially those that:
- align with [[1.0 Character Identity|Identity]] or your backstory,
- increase risk or consequence,
- or allow them to nudge the plot or scene to draw into meaningful developments.

**The GM may, if they wish, choose to treat enthusiastic or collaborative players to a reward of some kind.** This may be a little [[1.3 Advantage & Disadvantage|Advantage]] here, a little [[1.7 Impulse|Lingering Impulse]] there, a little leniency in an ability trigger somewhere else...

You are encouraged to lean into these suggestions when they feel right (but can always reject). Doing so often leads to stronger scenes, sharper drama, and more memorable outcomes. However, you always decide **how** your character acts. The GM may guide the direction, but YOU are responsible for the execution.
