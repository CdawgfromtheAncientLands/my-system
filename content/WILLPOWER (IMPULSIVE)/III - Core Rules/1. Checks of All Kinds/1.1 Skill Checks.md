*[[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]]s are a character’s main avenue of interaction with the world - a dice roll of 2d12, +/- [[1.1 Skill Checks|Modifier]]s, vs. [[1.2 Difficulty Class|Difficulty Class]].*
# Modifiers
Modifiers function in types and types and types. **Bonuses** are good and **Penalties** are bad. Most modifiers will be for [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]]s

Modifiers are expressed through +/- numbers or dice.
## So What Modifiers do I add?
1. [[1.2 Disciplines & Skills|Skill]] Bonus.
2. [[1.2 Disciplines & Skills|Discipline]] Bonus. 
3. Attribute Bonus. The attribute applied will depend on the Subskill being used.
4. Any external bonuses or penalties from items, circumstances, spells, or abilities.
# Success, Failure, & Degrees of Such
If you **meet or beat the [[1.2 Difficulty Class|Difficulty Class]]**, you succeed. If you don’t, you fail. 
> *Success and failure come in flavors depending on how close or far a check’s result is from the DC in either direction.* **Strict Outcomes** are used for game mechanics (such as Surgery or Attacks). **Narrative Outcomes** may be used for any non-codified Skill Check at GM discretion.
##### Strict Outcomes
Strict outcomes are the **mechanical bands** determined purely by the roll vs. DC. They power rules that call for fixed effects (e.g., damage, surgery steps, resource ticks) and are not subject to narrative negotiation. 

| <center></center>Comparison | Strict Outcome                         |
| --------------------------: | :------------------------------------- |
|      Less than DC by **7+** | **[[1.1 Skill Checks\|Crit Fail]]**    |
|                Less than DC | **[[1.1 Skill Checks\|Failure]]**      |
|            Meets / beats DC | **[[1.1 Skill Checks\|Success]]**      |
|          Beats DC by **7+** | **[[1.1 Skill Checks\|Crit Success]]** |
#### Snake Eyes & Boxcars
If you roll any combination of only 1s and 2s on 2d12, you automatically [[1.1 Skill Checks|Crit Fail]].  If both dice are either 11 or 12, you automatically [[1.1 Skill Checks|Crit Success]]. The likelihood of Boxcars is 1 in 144, so a good GM will make such an occurrence extra special.
# Narrative Outcomes
Narrative outcomes are the **fiction-facing bands** that describe how the situation changes in the story: backfire, shortfall, clean success, stylish success, etc. They guide GM consequences, opportunities, and follow-ups, and can coexist with strict effects at GM discretion. Use the narrative band to decide complications, boons, and positioning - even when no codified mechanic applies.

|      Comparison       | Narrative Outcome                                                                                                                                                           |
| :-------------------: | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|     **Crit fail**     | Your attempt backfires dramatically, worsening the situation or creating new obstacles. You may harm yourself, waste resources, or make the challenge harder going forward. |
|    **Fail by 3+**     | You fall well short, with clear negative consequences like wasted effort, lost resources, or drawing danger. The stench of it lingers in the air.                           |
|       **Fail**        | You miss the mark or achieve only part of your intent, often with complications; at best you avoid the very worst outcome.                                                  |
|      **Succeed**      | You achieve what you intend, more or less. If you only just meet the DC, there may be small complications along the way.                                                    |
| **Beat the DC by 3+** | You achieve what you intend with style and ease, perhaps earning notable bonuses or conveniences.                                                                           |
|   **Crit succeed**    | You achieve your goal with remarkable success, often gaining an unexpected boon. The outcome is swift, efficient, and leaves you in an advantageous position.               |
