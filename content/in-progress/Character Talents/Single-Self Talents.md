### **Master of the Now**
**Requirements:** [[Bold Selves|Bold]]
**Use:** 1/Session, refreshed when you earn a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]].
_You have an instinct for the exact moment when a situation is about flip._

When a situation is about to tip toward escalation or disaster, you may declare and perform a decisive action (no more than 1 Major + 1 Minor action in terms of mechanical effect) with priority of framing before the next exchange resolves. Roll 1d6:
- **1:** Your boldness is too brash - you stumble or stutter somewhat. Others see it coming and may react immediately, brace, or counter with full readiness. The impact is reduced, redirected, or complicated.
- **2-5:** You move with sharp timing and decisively seize the moment. Only those already on-guard, watching closely, or prepared for violence may meaningfully react (within the proper boundaries of the fiction), and suffer -1 on any [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]]s made to counteract you.
- **6:** You are the Master of the Now. You perform your course of action without a practical moment for others to stop, counteract, or even anticipate you. 

This Talent does not cancel or supersede existing rules for resolution, reactions, or outcomes, including [[1.1 Encounters|Turn]]s, [[1.1 Encounters|Round]]s, and [[1.2 Initiative|Initiative]].

### **Cut Open the Moment**
**Requirements:** [[Bold Selves|Bold]]
**Use:** 1/Session, refreshed when you earn a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]].  
_In dead silence and high tension, a precision blow lets the dogs of truth and action loose._

When someone stonewalls, stalls, or weasels - refuses to answer plainly or choose a side, you may force the issue by calling it out directly. Roll 1d6:
- **1-2:** Your push comes hot. The stall breaks, but painfully - denial hardens, tempers flare, or the truth emerges twisted or hostile. Deeper motivations and feelings remain hidden.
- **3–5:** You crack the façade. The target must respond clearly - by stating intent, taking an action, or revealing what they are avoiding. The scene moves forward decisively.
- **6:** You shatter the deadlock. Evasion collapses completely: the truth makes itself clear to all, a choice is made openly, or the situation flips into immediate action or resolution. Others react, sides may form, and consequences cascade immediately.

### **The Power of Unrelenting**
**Requirements:** [[Bold Selves|Bold]]
**Use:** 1/Session, refreshed every 2 [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]]s you earn.
_When you commit without flinching, the world makes room._

When something must be done, you get it done. *In character*, publicly declare a course of action that is attainable within the scene and fiction - like "We're getting out of here alive!!" or "I'll end you right now!". Roll 1d6.
- **1-3:** Your resolve burns hot and fast. You gain **1d2+1 uses** of the failure-reduction effect (as seen in **6** that last until **The Power of Unrelenting** would end.
- **4-5:** Your conviction carries weight. As **1-3**, but you additionally gain a single use of [[1.3 Advantage & Disadvantage|Advantage]] to be applied when you declare a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]].
- **6**: Your determination is great. On each of your [[1.1 Encounters|Turn]]s, when you suffer a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Failure]] or [[1.1 Skill Checks|Crit Fail]] on a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]] in pursuit of your goal, roll 1d4. On a 3 or 4, increase the check's [[1.1 Skill Checks|Strict Outcome]] by 1.

If you abandon, pause, or meaningfully compromise the declared course, the Talent’s effects end immediately. The Power of Unrelenting only carries so far as you maintain momentum.

### **Still Waters**
**Requirements:** [[Detached Selves|Detached]]
**Use:** 1/session, refreshed when you earn a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]]. 
*You are stoic in the moment - but the pain comes later.*

When you would suffer an instance of [[2.2 Stress|Stress]], you may ignore it. At the end of this [[1.1 Encounters|Encounter]] or scene (or at the beginning of next Rest, GM's call depending on context), immediately roll 1d6.
- **1-2**: Set your [[1.4 Secondary Attribute|Focus]] to 0 and immediately suffer a [[3. Breaking & Dying|Breakdown]]. This counts as a Harsh [[2.2 Stress|Stressor]] (3d4) to those around to witness it.
- **3-5:** Suffer double the negated [[2.2 Stress|Stress]]. If you suffer a [[3. Breaking & Dying|Breakdown]] due to this, it counts as a mild [[2.2 Stress|Stressor]] (2d4) to those around you.
- **6:** Suffer the negated [[2.2 Stress|Stress]].

[[2.2 Stress|Stress]] suffered from this effect cannot be mitigated in any way.

### **Like a Dead Fish**
**Requirements:** [[Detached Selves|Detached]]  
**Use:** 2 /session, refreshed when you earn a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]].  
_Those t._

When a creature attempts to intimidate, manipulate, guilt, provoke, or emotionally dominate you, you may deny the attempt entirely.  
That emotional leverage fails outright: you show no reaction, provide no reinforcement, and cannot be emotionally influenced by that creature for the remainder of the scene.

The aggressor is immediately forced onto the defensive. Their posture shifts from control to containment—hesitation, overcorrection, justification, or retreat.  
Until they meaningfully regain composure, their actions against you are reactive rather than assertive, and any further emotional escalation risks exposing insecurity, desperation, or loss of control to the scene.

_They reached for your nerves and came up empty._

### **Contagious Calm**
**Requirements:** [[Detached Selves|Detached]]  
**Use:** 1/session, refreshed when you earn a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]].  
_You kill drama by refusing to perform._

When a scene is particularly tense, a well-timed mundane activity shows you're not afraid - and lets people know to keep their tempers cool.


For the remainder of the moment, no emotional escalation may occur passively or by implication.  
Anyone who wishes to re-ignite tension must do so openly and decisively, accepting full narrative and social consequences for being the one who strikes the match.

_Drama starves when no one feeds it._
