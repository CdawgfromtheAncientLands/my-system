# <font color="#d83931">Ambient Noise</font>
Intense or chaotic sound can disrupt auditory actions, make communication difficult, and overwhelm creatures sensitive to noise.

| **Noise Level**         | **Effects**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Normal (0–69 dB)**    | No special effects.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| **Noisy (70–89 dB)**    | For every **20 ft** an **[[2. Action & Effect Traits\|{Auditory}]]** effect travels:<br>- The check to perceive or deliver it suffers -1<br>- Saving throws related to hearing gain +1 benefit.                                                                         |
| **Loud (89–109 dB)**    | As [[4. Ambient Effects\|Noisy]], but the effect occurs every 10ft instead of 20.                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| **Deafening (110+ dB)** | As [[4. Ambient Effects\|Loud]], but the effect occurs every 5ft instead of 10. Additionally:<br>- All creatures in the area are [[Condition\|Deaf]]. <br>- [[2. Action & Effect Traits\|{Concentrate}]] actions must pass a DC 3 [[Flat Check]] or automatically fail. |
# <font color="#d83931">Mood & Atmosphere</font>
Some environments feel soothing or oppressive. This affects how creatures recover **Vigor**, **Psyche**, and sometimes how Stress accumulates.

| **Level**        | **Mood Effects**                                                                                                                                   |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Soothing X**   | Recovered **Vigor** and **Psyche** increase by **2×X** per recovery event. Creatures _reduce_ **X Stress** per 10 minutes spent here.              |
| **Nice X**       | Recovered Vigor and Psyche increase by **X** per recovery event.                                                                                   |
| **Normal**       | No special effect.                                                                                                                                 |
| **Oppressive X** | Recovered Vigor and Psyche are **reduced** by **X** per recovery event.                                                                            |
| **Unsettling X** | Recovered Vigor and Psyche are reduced by **2×X**. Creatures **gain X Stress** per 10 minutes spent here. This cannot by itself cause a Breakdown. |

"Mood" represents environmental psychological pressure—deep forests, haunted ruins, sterile labs, buzzing cities, or idyllic glens.

# <font color="#d83931">Ambient Temperature</font>

Temperatures outside a creature’s comfort range can cause **Fatigue**, **Stress**, and direct damage.

|**Temperature Level**|**Effects**|
|---|---|
|**Deadly Cold** (–50°C or colder)|**1d4+4 Cold Damage** each round. Suffer **Strained +3**. Gain **Fatigued +1** for every minute spent. Uninhabitable wasteland.|
|**Greater Cold** (–49°C to –25°C)|**1d4+1 Cold Damage** every minute. Suffer **Strained +2**. Gain **Fatigued +1** every 10 minutes. Metal sticks to flesh; frostbite imminent.|
|**Bitter Cold** (–24°C to –11°C)|**1d4+1 Cold Damage** every 10 minutes. Suffer **Strained +2**. Gain **Fatigued +1** per hour. Breath freezes, skin necrotizes with exposure.|
|**Chilly Cold** (–10°C to 9°C)|Suffer **Strained +1**. Staying in the Lesser Cold zone for ~2 hours causes **Fatigue**. Frost forms, breath mists.|
|**Normal** (10°C to 29°C)|Ideal temperature. No special effects.|
|**Sweaty Heat** (30°C to 39°C)|Increase **Physical Stress** by **1–2** per prolonged labor or per ~6 hours without rest. Like a humid summer day.|
|**Blistering Heat** (40°C to 55°C)|**1d4+2 Heat Damage**, ignoring armor, every 10 minutes if Vigor has not recovered. Gain **Fatigued +1** every 2 hours. Skin burns.|
|**Hellish Heat** (46°C to 59°C)|**1d4+2 Heat Damage**, ignoring armor, every minute. Gain **Fatigued +1** every 10 minutes. Increase **Physical Stress +6–7**. Air burns; metal sears.|
|**Deadly Heat** (60°C or hotter)|**1d4+2 Heat Damage** each round, ignoring armor. Gain **Fatigued +1** every minute. Edge-of-a-lava-flow territory.|

## Additional Notes & GM Guidance

### **Projectiles**

For ranged attacks passing through liquid, mist, fog, or extreme heat/cold distortion, use **Storm Wind** rules as a baseline:

- increase DC per distance traveled
    
- apply penalties for viscosity, turbulence, or thermal disruption
    

### **Auditory Effects**

- Standard liquids count as **Noisy**
    
- Viscous liquids may count as **Loud** or **Deafening**
    

### **Visibility**

Apply your visibility/obscurement rules used for:

- fog
    
- rain
    
- heavy snowfall
    
- smoke
    

Liquids distort shapes at depth, while extreme heat creates shimmer and blur.


# <font color="#d83931">Light Levels</font>

Light conditions determine how creatures perceive their surroundings using **Sight**.

|**Level**|**Effects**|
|---|---|
|**Bright**|Anything in **Bright Light** can be seen clearly.|
|**Dim**|Objects and creatures in **Dim Light** can be no better than **Concealed** to creatures whose only **Precise Sense** is Sight.|
|**None**|In **No Light**, creatures can be no better than **Hidden** to creatures whose only **Precise Sense** is Sight.|

Light conditions may vary from hex to hex depending on terrain, weather, fog, vegetation, smoke, and magical or technological illumination.

# <font color="#d83931">Wind Speed</font>

Wind affects ranged attacks, movement, airborne creatures, and any loose objects. Higher speeds may require checks from creatures at risk of being blown off balance.

|**Wind Level**|**Wind Effects**|
|---|---|
|**Breezy**|No major effects.|
|**Gale (25+ mph)**|The area becomes **Noisy**. Apply **+10 DC** to non-ballistic ranged attacks. The air counts as **Difficult Terrain** while airborne. Small loose objects may be dragged.|
|**Storm (50+ mph)**|The area is **Loud**. Apply **+10 DC** to ballistic ranged attacks and **+20 DC** to non-ballistic ranged attacks. Tiny objects or creatures may be dragged; creatures may use **Labor** to resist.|
|**Low-Cat Hurricane (75+ mph)**|The area is **Loud**. Apply **+20 DC** to ballistic ranged attacks and **+30 DC** to non-ballistic ranged attacks. The air is **Difficult Terrain** while airborne. Creatures forced airborne must roll **+26 Gymnastics / Labor** or roll 1d4+1 on the Turbulence Table.|
|**Hi-Cat Hurricane (100+ mph)**|**Deafening**. Apply **+30 DC** to High-Velocity ranged attacks, **+60 DC** to Low-Velocity ranged attacks. Loose Medium or smaller objects are dragged. All airborne movement is treated as **Greater Difficult Terrain**. Any airborne creature must roll **+50 Gymnastics / Labor** or roll 2d4 on the Turbulence Table. Light structures suffer damage.|

Use common sense: anything not fixed down is at risk at 75+ mph winds.

# <font color="#d83931">Turbulence Table</font>

Whenever a creature is forced airborne in strong winds, roll on the table below if instructed by the wind level.

|**Roll**|**Result**|
|---|---|
|**1**|Nothing.|
|**2**|Thrown **1d3×5 ft** in a random direction, then movement continues.|
|**3**|Thrown **1d4×5 ft** in a random direction, then movement continues.|
|**4**|Thrown **2d3×5 ft** in a random direction, then movement continues.|
|**5**|Thrown **2d4×5 ft** in a random direction, then movement ends.|
|**6+**|Thrown **2d6×5 ft** in a random direction, then become **Prone** and begin falling (or colliding with obstacles as appropriate).|

Creatures thrown into objects may take collision damage (GM discretion based on distance).

# <font color="#d83931">Other Environmental Factors</font>

Environmental qualities beyond light, noise, heat, and terrain may influence Stress, visibility, or the difficulty of specific tasks.

- **Thin or Poor-Quality Air**  
    Causes nausea, exertion penalties, or Stress gain. Often makes an area **Oppressive**.
    
- **Strong Odors or Sickening Smells**  
    May impose penalties to Concentrate, Perception, or cause Stress in prolonged exposure.
    
- **Exceptional Humidity**  
    Increases effective ambient temperature; makes fire weak or ineffective; causes heavy fatigue.
    
- **Exceptional Dryness**  
    Encourages dehydration, cracked skin, static discharge, or makes environments **Unsettling/Oppressive**.
    
- **Deep Liquids**  
    High pressure crushes or injures creatures descending too quickly or too deeply.
    

These factors can be combined with Mood, Temperature, Noise, Visibility, and Terrain rules for more complex or extreme environmental encounters.

# <font color="#d83931">Rain & Snow</font>

Rain and snow affect movement, visibility, sound, and detection.

## Rainfall Levels

|**Level**|**Rainfall Effects**|
|---|---|
|**Drizzle**|Slight temperature drop. Mildly relaxing.|
|**Shower**|Decrease the **Detection Rating** of targets **40 ft or farther** from you by **1**.|
|**Downpour**|The area becomes **Noisy**. Decrease Detection Rating of targets **40 ft or farther** by **1**.|
|**Torrent**|**Noisy.** Decrease Detection Rating of targets **not adjacent** by **1**, and targets **20+ ft away** by **2**.|
|**Deluge**|**Loud.** Everything benefits from **Total Obscurement**. All terrain counts as **Difficult Terrain**.|

Rain always stacks with existing noise, visibility, and difficult terrain effects unless explicitly noted.

---

## Snowfall Levels

|**Level**|**Snowfall Effects**|
|---|---|
|**Light**|No meaningful effect.|
|**Moderate**|Decrease Detection Rating of targets **40 ft or farther** by **1**.|
|**Heavy**|Decrease Detection Rating of targets **not adjacent** by **1**, and **20+ ft away** by **2**.|
|**Whiteout**|Decrease Detection Rating of **all adjacent targets** by **1**, and non-adjacent creatures by **2**. All terrain becomes **Difficult**.|

Snow tends to be quieter than rain but far more visually disruptive.

# <font color="#d83931">Hailstones</font>

Hail intensity is defined by:

1. **Size** (damage on direct hit)
    
2. **Frequency** (chance of being struck each turn)
    

These two characteristics are **not linked**—hailstorms can have small stones falling constantly or giant stones falling rarely.

|**Level**|**Hail Size (Damage)**|**Frequency**|
|---|---|---|
|**Light**|1d3–1 Blunt or Blade|30% per turn. **Noisy.**|
|**Moderate**|1d4 Blunt or Blade|50% per turn. **Noisy.**|
|**Severe**|1d4+2 Blunt or Blade|75% per turn. **Noisy.**|
|**Deadly**|1d6+3 Blunt or Blade|100% per turn. **Noisy.**|

Creatures struck by hail suffer the listed damage. Armor may apply at GM discretion depending on shape, density, and speed.

# <font color="#d83931">GM Guidance</font>

### **Big Things & Small Weather**

Large creatures or structures (trolls, dragons, houses, vehicles) may be:

- unaffected by certain weather effects
    
- less affected by obscuring precipitation
    
- more affected by large hailstones
    

Apply common sense relative to scale and target profile.

### **Relativity of Conditions / Effects**

Think of weather as _force multipliers_. Depending on creature size, trait, or physiology, weather might:

- dampen hearing
    
- reduce scent
    
- obscure vision differently
    
- make one creature easy to see and another nearly invisible
    

A giant is easy to spot in a downpour; a mouse is impossible.

When in doubt, nudge effects toward clarity and tension rather than strict simulation.

Quicksand and thick mud, fire, electricity, lightning bolts, rocking and rolling, acid rain, falling rocks, different acids, effects of certain toxic gases
