Long-term travel is measured in days, and is a little bit loose in how it treats actual time-of-day or anything like that.
# Travel Actions
The core of long-term travel are [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]s - how much a creature can get done in a waking day, already accounting for things like...
- Breaking & Making camp
- Meals & Breaks
- Navigation
- Having a [[1.2 Rest|Long Rest]]. 

**A creature has 1 [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]] for every 10ft of its fastest [[1.4 Characteristics|Speed]].** A [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]] represents how much energy a creature has, how much time for rest they require, and how fast they can move at a sustained long-term pace.

Every day of travel, a creature may choose to push out an additional [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]] at the expense of one [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Fatigue]].

[[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Fatigue]] requires a **full day** of light/relaxed activity - no significant travel, no big fights - to recover from.
# What if creatures in a group have different speeds?
Generally, this can be played by ear.

The most common situations that bring up this question are...
- **We have a creature who's faster than everyone else!** Well, this one is simple. They have more [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]s per day, and may choose to go solo and scout, move ahead, or squeeze in some extra practice or crafting time while everyone else rests their feet.
- **We're riding mounts who are faster than us!** This one's a little more complicated. For the purposes of long-term travel, a mount may use ITS travel actions to move through hexes - but it costs ONE of the rider's [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]s for the mount to use TWO of its own for traversal.
# Beginning Travel Midday
If you've already had a bit of a day - then you decide to start travelling - the GM will adjudicate how many [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]'s worth of effort you've already spent doing whatever you've done. A good GM will warn you, when it matters, if you'd run out of time exploring somewhere. In general...
- Notable exploration of a dungeon/town - a few fights, a meal, a quick excursion - could cost 1-2 [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]s. 
- Popping in somewhere uncomplicated (visit a hermit and talk, explore a safe ruin) for 1-2 hours doesn't reduce [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]]s.
- Engaging in a threatening [[1.1 Encounters|Encounter]] costs ~1 [[2.2 Long-Term Travel|Travel Action]] - with "Threatening" being that it reasonably endangered a player's life or caused fair physical harm. A GM may rule that a party member who didn't engage much in said combat may not suffer the penalty.
