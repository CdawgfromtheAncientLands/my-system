## Identities

When creating a [[1.1 Player Characters|PC]], a player defines two narrative aspects of themselves: their [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Heart]] and [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Soul]].
## Heart
To put it simply, the [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Heart]] is their source of energy - *why* they do. 

Are they kind out of hatred for the cruel, or love for the meek? 
Do they follow the law because breaking it brought a terrible cost to them - or someone they cared about?

These are the kinds of questions the [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Heart]] hints to answering about a player character.

Additionally, it serves to hint at a character's instinctive reactions under pressure. When stakes rise, plans collapse, or pressure becomes deadly, a character must battle their own [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Heart]] for control of what to do - the character’s reactions may override their better judgment, values, or long-term goals.

A character's [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Heart]] doesn't have to be what they *want to be*. A black, doomed heart could belong to an incredibly kind [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Soul]], or a man in a dangerous profession may decide to be cruel out of kindness - to protect others through distancing himself.
## Soul
A [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Soul]] is a character’s guiding star - _what_ they believe in and _where_ they are trying to go. It answers the question of what a character holds meaningful enough to shape their choices, even when doing so is costly.

Do they believe in mercy because they have seen what vengeance becomes?  
Do they honor duty because chaos once took everything from them?  
Do they trust in luck, faith, or destiny because certainty has failed them before?

These are the kinds of convictions the [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Soul]] gestures toward.

Additionally, the Soul reflects how a character **tries** to act when given time, clarity, or the chance to choose. It is the framework they use to justify restraint, sacrifice, or resolve - the values they return to when attempting to hold themselves together under pressure.

A character’s [[1.1 Heart & Soul|Soul]] does not need to align cleanly with their Heart. A ruthless Heart may serve a merciful Soul. A fearful Heart may cling to an idealistic Soul. Much of a character’s struggle - and growth - comes from the effort required to keep belief aligned with instinct when the world refuses to cooperate.
## Heart & Soul Together
Every character lives in the space between Heart and Soul.

- The **Heart** supplies the energy and pressure.
- The **Soul** shapes how that energy manifests.

There will always be some overlap between the two concepts. Best not to think about it too strictly.

## Identity Tags
Each Heart and Soul is defined by a **Tag**, chosen from the following pairs:

> **[[Bold Selves|Bold]]** or **[[Tortured Selves|Tortured]]**  
> **[[Dutiful Selves|Dutiful]]** or **[[Free Selves|Free]]**  
> **[[Detached Selves|Detached]]** or **[[Empathetic Selves|Empathetic]]**  
> **[[Idealist Selves|Idealist]]** or **[[Skeptical Selves|Skeptical]]**  
> **[[Faithful Selves|Faithful]]** or **[[Pragmatic Selves|Pragmatic]]**

Although presented in opposing pairs, a character may choose opposing Tags between their Heart and Soul. In fact, many compelling characters do. Stories in _The Daring_ are not just "days in the life" - they are disruptions of the status quo.

---
## Identity Traits
After choosing Tags, a character assigns **2–4 descriptive traits** to each of their Identities.
Traits are written freely, in the player’s own words. They can describe:
- emotional triggers,
- beliefs,
- habits,
- fears,
- convictions,
- or recurring reactions.

Traits are **descriptive**, not mechanical.  
Only the **Heart or Soul Tag** carries rules weight.

---
## Identity Talents

A character’s chosen Identity Tags determine which identity talents they may select.

At character creation, a PC begins with **2 Identity Talents**. Additional Identity Talents may be gained, swapped, or recontextualized during play—typically during moments of intense narrative change, at the GM’s discretion.

People change!
