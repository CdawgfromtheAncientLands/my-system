*Skills are the muscle memory, knowledge, instincts, and techniques utilized in performing certain courses of action or pertaining to distinct subjects. They are the meat and cheese of this system.*

It may be painfully obvious, on occasion, that some skills are more generally-applicable than others. If this is the case, it is the adventure’s fault – but this may not even be a bad thing! Be sure to understand what skills are most useful where, and which ones should be the focus of a campaign.
#### <u><font color="#ff0000">Score</font></u>
A skill's [[1. Skill|Skill Score]] is a flat number added to every check with a skill. 
#### <u><font color="#ff0000">Proficiencies</font></u>
A [[Proficiency]] is a flat bonus to a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]] applied under certain circumstances as a specialist "subskill" within its parent skill area.

| **Level** | **Bonus** |
| --------- | --------- |
| Untrained | N/A       |
| *Novice*  | +1        |
| *Adept*   | +2        |
| *Expert*  | +3        |
| *Master*  | +4        |
#### <font color="#ff0000"><u>Linked Attribute</u></font>
Every skill has one or more Linked Attributes that contribute their [[1.3 Primary Attribute|Attribute Modifier]] to the [[1.1 Skill Checks]]. In the case of several Linked Attributes, a player may choose whichever they prefer, or the GM may say whichever one is relevant to the situation – but a check will NOT benefit from 2 Linked Attributes at once.
### <u><font color="#ff0000">Cost Bands</font></u>
Not all Skills are made equal, especially when an adventure calls for a very specific skillset. Skills will have different [[Experience]] costs for increases, organized into different classes: Powerful, Standard, and Flavor.
- A <u>Standard</u> skill is something that is more useful than not but still has plenty of shortcomings in an adventure. Observation, Medicine, and Body skills are good examples of Standard. In an ideal campaign, nearly all skills should be Standard.
- <u>Powerful</u> skills are skills that prove to be near-universally applicable, being able to solve many of the problems a party faces. In a social adventure, Influence and Subterfuge skills may be powerful. In a battle-focused adventure, Melee and Ranged would be Powerful skills that the players would often find themselves relying on. The key component of a skill being Powerful is that other obviously viable options are being disregarded in favor of it, or that all players are even demanded to use it by some aspect of the adventure.
- <u>Flavor</u> skills are those that have little easy or obvious use in an adventure. These skills may serve to fill out one’s repertoire and provide good opportunities for “fleshing-out” a character’s abilities to make them feel more full-bodied.

| Skill Score | Exp. cost | Max proficiency |
| ----------- | --------- | --------------- |
| 0           |           | Adept           |
| +1          |           | Expert          |
| +2          |           | Master          |
| +3          |           | Legend          |
The Exp. cost listed in the table is per point when raising the skill to a number within that score band. For example, raising a Standard skill from 4 to 5 costs 6 Exp., while raising it from 12 to 13 (crossing into Expert) costs 20 Exp.
