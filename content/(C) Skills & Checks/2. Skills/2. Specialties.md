_Specialties represent a character’s hyper-focused knowledge, niche experience, or cultivated background expertise—too specific to warrant a full Skill, but meaningful enough to grant an edge when that exact subject comes up._

A Specialty never replaces a [[1. Skill|Primary Skill]] or [[1. Skill|Subskill]].  
Instead, it **adds a small, precise bonus** when it directly applies. Specialties reward players who think carefully about their characters’ histories, obsessions, trades, or odd little fascinations.

Specialties are at their best when they appear rarely, but memorably—  
_the exact moment a character’s odd little background suddenly becomes the perfect tool for the job._

Specialties are deliberately narrow.  
A Specialty in _Smithing_ does not apply to _metalworking in general_.  
A Specialty in _Egyptology_ does not apply to _ancient history broadly_.  
A Specialty in _Karate_ does not apply to _all unarmed combat_.  
## **Using Specialties**
When a character makes a Skill Check, the GM may allow a relevant Specialty to apply. A Specialty bonus stacks with the check’s usual formula:

> **Attribute Mod + Primary Skill Score + Subskill Score + Specialty Bonus**

If the Specialty’s relevance is unclear, err on the side of narrow application. Specialties should never outshine full [[1. Skill|Skill]]s or [[1. Skill|Subskill]]s, and they should never become universal advantages.
## **Specialty Ranks & Bonuses**
Specialties have only two mechanical tiers. 

| Rank           | Bonus | EXP Cost from Previous |
| -------------- | ----- | ---------------------- |
| **Trained**    | +1    | 3 XP                   |
| **Specialist** | +2    | 6 XP                   |
# **Types of Specialties**
## **Language Specialty**
A Language Specialty determines fluency. Language Specialties do **not** add numeric bonuses to Skill Checks unless the GM rules an extremely specific situation warrants it (e.g., interpreting a double entendre or recognizing a coded phrase).
- **Trained** — You can hold simple conversations, read basic text, and get your point across with effort.
- **Specialist** — You are fluent: capable of nuanced dialogue, proper grammar, idioms, and comfortably reading or writing complex ideas.

Example languages: _English, Spanish, Venusian, Old Imperial, Rivertongue_, etc.
## **Martial Arts Specialty**
A Martial Arts Specialty represents learned techniques from a formal style such as _Sambo, Karate, Muay Thai, Wing Chun,_ or others. Martial Arts Specialties **do not** add bonuses to attack rolls. Instead, they act as **prerequisites** for certain feats, stances, or advanced maneuvers that reference that style.
- **Trained** — You understand the fundamentals of the style.
- **Specialist** — You can apply the style’s core principles consistently and effectively.
## **Knowledge Specialty**
A Knowledge Specialty applies when recalling knowledge about, analyzing, or performing tasks related to a very narrow subject. It does **not** override broader Skills like _Lore, Observation, Survival,_ or _Artifice_—it only sharpens a thin slice of them.
Examples include:
- **Smithing**
- **Accounting**
- **Appraisal**
- **Lip-Reading**
- **Bicycling**
- **Boating**
- **Interpretive Dance**
- **Knot-Tying**
- **Cartography**
- **Egyptology**
- **Floristry**
- **Gambling**
- **Sculpture**
- **Fiction Genres**
- **Farming Practices**
**Trained** — You can perform or identify the basics of this niche.
**Specialist** — You operate comfortably and confidently within the niche, spotting nuances others miss.
## **Instrument Specialty**
An Instrument Specialty represents proficiency with a specific musical instrument. These Specialties add their bonus to relevant **Arts & Expression** or performance-related checks.
Examples: _Guitar, Oboe, Waterphone, Sousaphone, Xylophone, Double Bass, French Horn._
- **Trained** — You can play the instrument competently and perform simple pieces.
- **Specialist** — You can perform challenging compositions, improvise, and express fine control over tone and timing.
