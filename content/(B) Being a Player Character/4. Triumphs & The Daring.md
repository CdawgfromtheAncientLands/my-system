# <font color="#ffff00">Triumphs</font>
<font color="#ffff00">Triumphs</font> are awarded to each member party after accomplishing objectives. The most basic example of this is the completion of a challenging combat encounter, worth 1 <font color="#ffff00">Triumph</font>. 

When a [[1.1 Player Characters|PC]] is rewarded a [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]], they may refresh the per-session uses of ONE of their [[Single-Self Talents]] or [[Multi-Self Talents]].

Triumphs convert into Experience during Long Rests, which is spent on increasing [[1. Skill|Skill Score]]s.
### Awarding Triumphs
# **1. Session-Level Expectations**
Over the course of a typical session, you’re aiming for:
- 1-3 Triumphs total for the whole party in a low-stakes session
- 3–6 Triumphs total in a high-stakes or climactic session
## **2. Category-Based Awarding (the core engine)**
### **A. Confronting Major Danger**
Award **1 Triumph** when the party overcomes a conflict that _mattered_:
- A fight where someone risked death, trauma, or loss of something that mattered,
- A rival whose agenda advanced unless stopped,
- A monster that forced tactical adaptation,
- A target they were not expected to beat easily.
If a combat or danger is NOT integral to the plot, difficult to overcome, or meaningful to the characters, no Triumph is to be awarded for it.
### **B. Turning the Tables**
Award **1 Triumph** for _solving_ a problem in a way that flips the situation:
- Clever tactics that bypass a strong enemy,
- Turning a dead-end into a new lead,
- Taking an NPC who was an obstacle and making them an asset.
- Gaining a powerful ally or discovering an incredible item/place/thing
### **C. Accomplishing a Significant Goal**
Award **1 Triumph** when the party completes:
- A major quest step or contract milestone,
- A mission objective that changes what comes next,
- A hard logistical or environmental challenge.
- Uncovers a significant or powerful connection that changes the story 
### **D. Foiling or Advancing a Plot**
Award **1 Triumph** when the party materially alters a faction’s or villain’s trajectory:
- Sabotaging a scheme,
- Revealing a critical truth,
- Completing a ritual,
- Preventing a disaster,
- Advancing their own faction’s agenda.
### **E. Heroic Risk With Impact**
Rarely, award **1 Triumph** when a player does something:
- Genuinely risky,
- Genuinely consequential,
- And it works.
# <font color="#de7802">Daring</font>
[[1.1 Player Characters|Player Character]]s are [[The Daring]]. They are people (or creatures) full of flavor and motivation, embarking on an adventure - regardless of how hard it pushes them, prepared to climb the highest mountain and delve the deepest pits.

At the beginning of a PCs turn in an encounter, they gain [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Daring]] equal to the amount of [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Triumph]]s they have banked.

A GM may choose to award [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Daring]] during an encounter if [[1.1 Player Characters|PCs]] push into uncertainty, take risks, or seize momentum creatively - regardless of how successful their attempts are. Climactic combats should be generous with this.
### Inspired Daring
When a player is bold, inventive, or sharply engaged with the story, the GM can award **Inspired Daring** (or Inspiration) - a steadier, banked form of nerve that persists beyond any single moment of crisis. 
A PC can only have 2 points of [[4. Triumphs & The Daring|Inspired Daring]] at a time, and they reset when the GM says so - end-of-session, end-of-arc, next long rest, whatever - depending on the importance of the situation they were gained in.



At the beginning of every round in an [[1.1 Encounters|Encounter]]

Daring can be spent to pay [[1.4 Secondary Attribute|Focus]] costs in a 1:1 ratio, but can also be spent as a unique resource for certain abilities or to upgrade standard actions.

At the beginning of a [[1.1 Player Characters|Player Character]]'s [[Turn]] in an [[Encounter]], they gain 1 [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Daring]] for every [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Triumph]] they have stored (and lose any [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Daring]] leftover from the previous turn).
#### Temporary Daring
GMs are encouraged to reward high-performing, engaged, risk-taking, or creative players with [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Temp. Daring]]. This kind of [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Daring]] doesn't go away, allowing usage outside of encounters or after the party just converted some Experience.

A [[1.1 Player Characters|Player Character]] can have up to 3 [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Temp. Daring]] at a time. 

A good GM uses uses [[4. Triumphs & Daring|Temp. Daring]] to build anticipation, reward high engagement during/before focused or intense moments, and encourage PCs to keep moving.
