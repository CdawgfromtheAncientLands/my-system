A player character needs a few things:

1. **Values** - you know, the standard they hold themselves to. It's best to start with a general list of "What's good, what's bad" based on your character's backstory. To be the best you can be, try writing a 3-4 of them down!
2. **A [[1. Character Identity|Heart]] tag and two Heart traits (actionable compulsions) related to it.** These should be easy to choose and write based off your backstory and the values your character starts out with.
3. **A [[1. Character Identity|Soul]] tag and two Soul traits (perceived external truths) related to it.** Badaboom!
# On Playing With Identity
You don't NEED to know exactly what you're doing, and you don’t need to get this perfect. **Identity** is an acting exercise, meant to draw your character (and you) into situations that you might never think of by yourself - it helps you put on the mask, and get some REALLY good roleplay.

Your **Identity** isn’t a script. It’s what your character believes is _right_. That belief is going to push on you, especially when things get tense. Most of the time, it’s just there in the background. If you’re stuck between choices, it's a great thing to use as a tiebreaker. We make compromises and contradictions with ourselves every day, and a person is rarely so fully actualized that they act exactly as they envision or want.

But when things get heated? That’s when it stops being subtle.
### Identity SHOULD win… but it doesn’t have to.
If your Identity is pulling you in a direction, you should _feel_ that. That’s the point - but nothing is stopping you from going against it. If your heart tells you to kill the villain in front of you, but the party yells at you to spare them... it's YOUR choice to listen to them or not! But, as seen in [[3.2 Stress]], contradicting yourself brews inner turmoil (which is really awesome).
### Good play isn’t “optimal”
Good play is:
- making the call your character would make,
- even when it’s messy,
- even when it makes things worse,
- or being forced into the “right” move and _hating it_.

If your Identity never causes problems, then you are NOT fully playing the game. Either:
- the GM isn’t putting you in situations that actually matter to you
- or you’re not pushing your character forward when it counts.
### Contradictions are where things get good
Your **Heart** and **Soul** don’t have to agree. When they clash, that IS a problem... but it's also excellent character-building! You can:
- pick one and deal with the fallout,
- try to satisfy both and possibly earn some [[1.7 Force of Will|Lingering Force]],
- or lean into the tension and let it **explode**!

If you _do_ manage to line them up in a meaningful way? That’s AWESOME! That’s when your character is acting with real conviction, and those are some great moments.

Want to skip out on all the inner turmoil? Remember - you can choose your character's identity, so just make sure you agree with yourself.
### Traits aren’t commands
Your traits aren’t telling you what you _have_ to do - they’re telling you what your character _believes_ is right.
### You are more than a few words
Identity is the core of your character, but not the whole thing. As a player, it's your job to fill in the gaps, interpret your traits and build bridges between them.  
# For GMs (quick, but important)
Give players something to push against. Don’t just put “interesting stuff” in the world... put _their stuff_ in front of them.
- Hit their values.
- Challenge their beliefs.
- Apply a bit of **Stress** when they struggle or suppress what they feel.

And if a player isn’t engaging? Don’t wait. Aim directly at them.
# MOST IMPORTANTLY, REMEMBER - WINNING IS WHEN EVERYBODY IS HAVING FUN!!!
