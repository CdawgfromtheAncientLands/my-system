As PCs achieve victory in their endeavors, they gain Fervor!
*Fervor is a bit of a generic term. You can change it to something like Confidence, Impulse, Nerve, Heroism... anything that fits the genre.*

> Just saved the duke from a band of scrappy kidnappers? +1 Fervor!
> 
> Just shot your age-old bounty-hunting rival through the forehead? Well, make sure he's dead first. Then, +1 Fervor!
> 
> Your kleptomaniacal companion just filched the sacred gem of Shangri-La? Fervor!... even if you didn't help him. Your companion's victories are your victories, too (for fairness' sake).
# Awarding Fervor
*This section sets up expectations for GMs and players. As the awarding of Fervor could never be effectively handled by hard rules, consulting this section is NECESSARY to understand this game's intention.*
### 1. Session-Level Expectations
Over the course of a typical session, you’re aiming for:
- 0-2 Fervor total for the whole party in a low-stakes or slow session
- 3–6 Fervor total in a high-stakes or climactic session
### 2. What Counts as "Fervor"?
*A singular Fervor* should be awarded when the party:
- **Overcomes meaningful danger**  
    A conflict with real stakes, risk, or resistance that required effort to overcome. One or more party members ending the encounter near-death (or dead) is a good marker for this.
- **Completes a significant objective**  
    A quest step, mission goal, or major obstacle that changes what comes next.
- **Alters the course of a plot or faction**  
    Disrupting plans, revealing truths, preventing outcomes, or advancing an agenda.
- **Succeeds at a high-risk, high-impact action** _(rare)_  
    A bold move with real consequences that gives all notions of likelihood and fate the what-for.
### Fervor Quests
A single event should NOT yield more than one Fervor unless the GM REALLY thinks so. A good GM may let the party know, beforehand, how many Fervor a core objective is worth - "Saving your hometown from the Orc invasion is worth 3 Fervor." 

Think of it like an in-game quest, set to give the players something to slaver over or look forward to. Multi-Fervor objectives may have their Fervor awarded incrementally - "We successfully avoided notice and poisoned their water", "We convinced a local bandit gang to help us fight", "We defeated the Orc chief!"

And then, if you have a kind GM, they may award you one more Fervor during the banquet held in your honor - in return for the great renown you've gained by becoming heroes of the land. A good GM always manages to make the players feel the greatness of their accomplishments.
# Utilizing Fervor
Fervor, with rest and reflection, transform into [[1.9 Levels & Progression|Experience]] and [[1.5 Character Talents|Talent]]! They are how a Player Character learns and grows into something greater.

## Relativity of Fervor
Not every victory matters forever. As your character grows, what once felt significant may become routine. Early on, earning influence in a local guild might be a hard-won and meaningful achievement - worthy of a [[1.6 Fervor|Fervor]]. But repeating that same feat again and again, even if it still requires effort, may no longer carry the same weight.

**Fervor are not awarded for difficulty alone... they are awarded for impact.**

To earn a Fervor, an action must meaningfully change your situation, advance your goals, or affect the world around you in a way that matters _right now_. Doing something impressive but irrelevant, repetitive, or disconnected from the current stakes (even if it is risky or difficult) may not result in a Fervor.

For players, this means:
> **Focus on what matters.**  

Your greatest rewards come from pursuing new & meaningful goals, not just overcoming challenges.

For GMs, this means:
> **Judge significance, not effort.**  
> Ask: _Did this change the direction of the story, the party’s position, or what comes next?_ If not, it likely isn’t a Fervor - no matter how hard it was.

As the scope of play expands, so too does the scale of what counts as meaningful. Fervor grow with the story... and with you.
