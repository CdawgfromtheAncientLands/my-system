**Stress** represents mounting psychological pressure: fear, doubt, shock, and emotional strain. It builds when you’re pushed beyond your comfort, face danger, or fail when it matters. As Stress rises, you become less steady, less reliable, and closer to breaking.

Stress is not random. It comes from:
- **Danger** (threats, violence, the unknown)
- **Failure** (especially at critical moments)
- **Exposure** (time spent in hostile or unsettling environments)
- **Personal stakes** (your fears, beliefs, and relationships)

If your [[3.2 Stress|Stress]] rises higher than your current [[1.1 Attributes|Willpower]]... things get messy.
# When Does Stress Happen?

Stress should follow a simple rule:
> **If the moment would make a reasonable person tense, afraid, or shaken... assign Stress.**

But more importantly:
> **Players should be able to commonly predict when Stress is likely.**

A good GM will use [[3.2 Stress|Stress]] to reinforce:
- Entering meaningful danger
- Escalation of stakes
- Consequences of failure
- Emotional or narrative turning points,

And AVOID:
- Random or constant application with no pattern (or for things the players don't care about)
- Punishing players for normal play
- Assigning Stress without clear fictional cause

Players are not passive recipients of Stress. They can and should think about:
- **Avoiding it** (caution, preparation, smart play)
- **Accepting it** (push forward despite risk)
- **Spending resources to mitigate it** (Endurance, abilities, positioning)
- **Choosing when to risk escalation** (high-reward actions, narrative choices)

# Stressors

| Stressor  | When to Use It                                                                                  | Amount     |
| --------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Harsh     | Uncomfortable - more than just annoying. You hesitate to advance.                               | 1d4        |
| Severe    | Immediate danger or meaningful failure. Something has gone quite wrong.                         | 2d4 to 3d4 |
| Traumatic | Major turning points, trauma, or overwhelming fear. Doom is on the horizon, the worse has come. | 4d4+       |
Here are some discrete examples that should ALWAYS trigger [[3.2 Stress|Stressor]]s. A single event should not count as multiple [[3.2 Stress|Stressor]]s - One event, one stressor.
#### Harsh
- Beginning a meaningful combat [[1.1 Encounters|Encounter]] OR being ambushed
- [[1.1 Skill Checks|Crit Fail]]ing a [[1.1 Skill Checks|Skill Check]] of any kind
- Suffering a [[Critical Hit]]
- Sudden and shocking brutality
- An ally suffers a [[3.3 Mortal Peril|Mental Break]]
- You or an ally suffers [[3.3 Mortal Peril|Injury]]
- You act in direct contradiction of your [[1. Character Identity|Heart]] or [[1. Character Identity|Soul]] (GM may make this more severe)
#### Severe
- An ally begins [[3.3 Mortal Peril|Dying]] (2d4)
- Horrific or gut-wrenching brutality
#### Traumatic
- An ally dies.
# Out-of-box Stress Distribution
The wonder of the Stress system is that it allows for building tension, tradeoffs between power and safety, and the expression of social/environmental factors in a way that truly matters to a PC's effectiveness and well-being. 

The issue is that it's almost entirely GM-specific. The game WILL work well if a GM chooses not to apportion any Stress beyond enemy abilities and Stress Costs, but it works BEST when the stakes are expressed through all available avenues.
